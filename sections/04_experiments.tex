\section{Evaluation Protocol}
\label{sec:evaluation}

\subsection{Compared Models}
\label{subsec:compared_models}

We compare five model classes. The uncalibrated aerodynamic model reports the raw
model error before fitting the calibration parameters. The calibrated
aerodynamic model shows how far the bounded physical calibration can reduce that
error. The direct Transformer predicts the full effective wrench with no explicit
aerodynamic-model term. The calibrated model plus residual MLP tests whether
instantaneous onboard variables can explain the remaining force and moment
residuals. The channel-aware hybrid Transformer tests whether causal history
improves residual correction while using calibrated-model residual correction for
forces and direct neural prediction for moments.

Together, the models separate the effects of physical calibration, residual
learning, temporal history, and channel-specific output semantics. The direct
Transformer serves as a black-box full-wrench reference. The residual models show
how much accuracy is retained when the calibrated aerodynamic model remains
explicit, and the channel-aware model tests whether force and moment channels
benefit from different prediction semantics.

\subsection{Train/Validation/Test and Cross-Validation}
\label{subsec:split_protocol}

The primary experiment uses the fixed whole-log split described in Sec.~\ref{subsec:canonical_samples}. The aerodynamic-model calibration uses training logs only. Neural models are trained on training logs, selected using validation logs, and evaluated on held-out test logs. Normalization statistics, calibration parameters, and early-stopping decisions are not fitted on test logs.

To check whether the result depends on one particular test composition, we also run five-fold whole-log cross-validation with approximately date-stratified and sample-balanced folds. Date stratification is used to make the evaluation less sensitive to date-specific variations in weather, wind condition, and airframe condition, and to better assess generalization across flight dates. In fold $k$, log fold $k$ is used for testing, log fold $(k+1) \bmod 5$ is used for validation, and the remaining folds are used for training. The channel-aware hybrid Transformer configuration is fixed across folds. The stratification is approximate because the number of available logs differs across flight dates.

\subsection{Metrics}
\label{subsec:metrics}

The primary metrics are per-target root-mean-square error (RMSE) and mean absolute error (MAE):
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{RMSE}_{j}
  =
  \sqrt{
    \frac{1}{N}
    \sum_{n=1}^{N}
    \left(
      \hat{y}_{n,j} - y_{n,j}
    \right)^2
  },
  \label{eq:rmse}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{MAE}_{j}
  =
  \frac{1}{N}
  \sum_{n=1}^{N}
  \left|
    \hat{y}_{n,j} - y_{n,j}
  \right|.
  \label{eq:mae}
\end{equation}
Aggregate RMSE and MAE are reported as compact summaries, but per-target values are the main evidence because the six wrench channels have different units, amplitudes, excitation levels, and label quality.

To give the absolute errors a scale reference, we also report the held-out
standard deviation $\sigma_{y,j}$ of each target channel and the normalized
error $\mathrm{RMSE}_j/\sigma_{y,j}$. We report $R^2$ as a secondary metric.
Because the six channels have different scales and excitation levels, $R^2$ is
interpreted together with RMSE, MAE, and $\mathrm{RMSE}/\sigma_y$. This is
especially important for moment channels, where the label variance is much
smaller than in the force channels.
