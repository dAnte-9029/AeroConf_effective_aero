\begin{abstract}
Accurate force and moment models are needed for flapping-wing simulation, control design, and flight-log interpretation, but physics-based models often leave systematic errors when applied to outdoor flight of a complete vehicle. This paper studies real-flight effective-wrench modeling for a bird-scale flapping-wing vehicle. We reconstruct six-component body-frame effective wrench labels from outdoor PX4 flight logs using measured mass, center-of-gravity, and inertia properties, and align the labels with a DeLaurier-style simulator prior. Instead of replacing the prior with an unrestricted black-box model, we fit a structured correction from simulator-available variables including wingbeat phase, airdata, flapping frequency, body rates, and control commands. On held-out whole-log tests, the phase-structured correction reduces force-channel RMSE relative to the affine-aligned prior by $68.3\%$ for $f_{x,b}$, $15.0\%$ for $f_{y,b}$, and $69.8\%$ for $f_{z,b}$. Effective moment prediction is also reported, with the strongest moment-channel result in $m_{y,b}$ ($R^2=0.798$), while $m_{x,b}$ and $m_{z,b}$ remain lower-SNR channels. Residual diagnostics show that the main force-channel mismatch contains repeatable wingbeat-phase, frequency, and flight-condition structure, and that the correction removes much of this structured discrepancy. These results support log-conditioned correction of a low-dimensional flapping-wing simulator prior as a prerequisite step toward simulator embedding, while replay and closed-loop simulator validation remain future work.
\end{abstract}
